# A data checklist from ISO and friends, cut down to one person

Scope: the user suggested that ISO standards could give the AI a good checklist for data security, backups and so on. This note reads what the standards actually say (as far as their public pages allow), keeps the parts that fit one person building small local tools (sometimes shared with a few colleagues, sometimes a small private website), maps them to what the workbench already does, names the gaps, and ends with a checklist of 12 items the AI works through. Research date 2026-09-30.

Tags: `[LOCAL]` = observed on this machine or in this repo. `[INFERENCE]` = my reasoning. `[UNVERIFIED]` = not confirmed in a primary source. `[2nd]` = a secondary source (not the standards body). ISO texts are paywalled: what I could read is ISO's own catalogue pages. The Online Browsing Platform preview needs a browser session that was not available (the relay was not connected), and several ISO pages answered 403 on a first try. **Every quote of a control's wording below comes from a consultancy or ISACA page that quotes ISO/IEC 27002:2022; the recommendations do not depend on the exact words.**

## Short answer

- ISO/IEC 27001 and 27002 are written for an organisation that runs a management system. ISO itself says 27001 lets an organisation adapt to "their size and needs" [1] and that 27002 "cannot be certified to" [2]. So they are a menu, not a checklist to complete. About 15 of the 93 controls in 27002:2022 touch what a workbench tool does [INFERENCE from the list in 3]. Everything about policies, audits, training, suppliers, physical security and certification does not fit one person.
- The best small-scale sources are free and shorter: CIS Controls v8.1 (Control 3 data protection, Control 11 data recovery), the UK NCSC's small-organisation backup page, NIST CSF 2.0 and its small-business guide. They say the same four things ISO does: know what data you hold; keep it only as long as needed; keep a copy in a separate place; prove you can restore it [8.13 in 4; 17; 21; 22].
- The workbench already does most of the doing (originals read-only, dated backups, data versions, tested restore code, private-first websites). The real gaps are few: the backups sit on the same disk as the data; restore is proven for the code but never for this tool's own data; automatic backups are only taken when the tool starts; nothing says how long other people's personal data may stay, and labelled backups are never deleted; and nothing records what a tool holds or where anything leaves.
- Result: 12 checks. Seven run for every tool that keeps data (silently). Five apply only when other people's data, a company, sharing or a website is involved. The user sees a sentence in four places only (the copy elsewhere, how long to keep other people's data, the fields to keep, and any check that fails).

### GDPR, briefly, for a person processing others' data

- Own use: the regulation does not apply to processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity" (Art. 2(2)(c)) [25]. The user's own job hunt is outside it. Nothing to say, ever, as [R1] already has it.
- At work: the company "determines the purposes and means" (Art. 4(7)), so it is the controller; a person acting under its authority may only process on its instructions (Art. 29) [25]. The user's company's rules beat the AI's defaults. That is exactly the existing "Whose data" flow and the forwardable message [R1].
- Duties that reach the tool's design, all the company's: keep data only as long as necessary (Art. 5(1)(e)), only what is needed (5(1)(c)), and accurate (5(1)(d)) [25]; tell people the storage period (Art. 13(2)(a)) [25]; security that includes "the ability to restore the availability and access to personal data in a timely manner" and "a process for regularly testing" the measures (Art. 32(1)(c), (d)) [25]; report a breach "not later than 72 hours" after becoming aware, unless unlikely to risk anyone (Art. 33(1)) [25].
- Records of processing (Art. 30) are waived under 250 staff unless the processing is "not occasional" or has special categories (Art. 30(5)) [25]. A regular hiring process is probably not occasional, so the company may need an entry; the tool's NOTES lines (items 1, 6, 10) are what IT would need to write it [INFERENCE].
- Backups and deletion: when erasure applies, the ICO says the key is to put backup data "beyond use" until it is overwritten on a schedule, and to be clear about it [26]. That is a UK regulator on UK GDPR; the EU text is the same idea [INFERENCE].
- The user's country is unknown. GDPR is used as the best-known example; other countries have similar laws [UNVERIFIED]. A club or volunteer group with other people's data is not obviously "household"; treat it like the work case and ask whoever runs the group [INFERENCE].
- Text read from gdpr-info.eu, an unofficial mirror of Regulation (EU) 2016/679; EUR-Lex was blocked by a bot check [25].

## Recommendations

### A. The checklist

The AI does these itself, in this order of milestones. A line to the user appears only where the last column says so. Nothing is a briefing; each check is silent when it passes.

**Always (every tool that keeps its own data or writes files for the user)**

| # | The check, in plain words | When the AI runs it | What it produces / what the user sees | From |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What does this tool hold, and could it be rebuilt from the user's original files? Kinds only ("bills: supplier, date, amount"), never contents | When the shape is agreed (`build.md` milestone 2); again whenever a field is added and at every 5th change (see C) | NOTES "Data" gets `Holds:` and `Rebuild from originals: yes / no / partly`. Says nothing to the user. The answer sets how much effort items 3 and 4 need: rebuildable data needs no more than the automatic copy | 27002 5.9 [4]; CSF ID.AM-07 [17]; CIS 3.2 [21] |
| 2 | Is there an automatic copy, and does it keep happening while the tool is open? | Before the first real data (the `data-safety` block goes in then); at every 5th change | NOTES "Backups:" gets the path and the worst case in plain words: "if the disk works, you lose at most what you typed since the last copy, about a day of use". One line to the user only if the tool gets the new timer ("It now saves a copy each day it's open, and when you close it"). Today the worst case is unbounded for a tool left open (see C1) | 8.13 [4]; PR.DS-11 [17]; CIS 11.2 [21]; NCSC "automatic" [22]; RPO defined as "the point in time to which data must be recovered after an outage" [19] |
| 3 | Can a copy actually be brought back and opened, for this tool's own data? | Once before the first real data (with a sample-sized copy); after every change to how data is stored; once about every 3 months of active use when the data cannot be rebuilt (item 1) | Run the existing `copy` then open the tool on it (the "Go back" recipe [R2]); compare row or file counts with the live data; delete the Temp copy. ### B. What the user sees

At most these four sentences, each once per tool and each with a suggestion: the copy elsewhere (item 4), the fields to keep (8), how long to keep them (9), and the company message (10, already in the skill). Everything else is a NOTES line or a test. A failed check gets one line: what was wrong, what the AI did, that the data is fine or what is at stake. The words "standard", "control", "ISO", "policy", "retention" and "RPO" never appear; the user's own words from `CONTEXT.md` do ("resumes", "shortlist") [09 rec 3].

### C. Where each piece goes in the skill (no edits made in this task)

1. `blocks/data-safety` [R2]: (a) call `backupIfDue(20)` from a once-an-hour timer and on quit, so the worst case is about a day of use even for a tool left open (item 2; whether a full copy each hour is cheap for large data folders is unmeasured, the block itself says so [R2]); (b) an optional "extra copy folder" setting plus a "Save a copy elsewhere" button (native folder picker) that writes the same copy there, remembers the date, and shows one small notice on start when the last outside copy is old and the data has changed; the notice age (30 days) is my number [INFERENCE]; (c) a `prune` command that deletes labelled and automatic copies older than a limit, used only when `Keep until` exists; (d) a `verify` command that runs `copy`, opens the copy, checks integrity and row or file counts, and prints the line for NOTES (item 3).
2. `safety.md` "Data": one sentence on the extra copy folder (the line "It's local and never synced" becomes "never synced unless the user picks a synced folder for the extra copy, and then the AI says so in a line"); the retention rule in "Whose data"; the checklist as a short list that points to the NOTES lines, not a copy of this table.
3. `starters/NOTES.md` "Data" section: add the lines `Holds`, `Rebuild from originals`, `Copy elsewhere`, `Restore checked`, `Leaves this computer`, `Keep until`, `Who can open it`, `Who to tell`, `Data lines checked`. Kinds only, in line with the rule that NOTES hold no contents [R1].
4. `build.md` milestones: items 1, 6, 8, 9, 10 at "the shape"; 5 at "the hard part"; 2, 3, 4, 7 at "thinnest working tool"; 11, 12 at the first share or deploy. Every 5th change (counted from the save points), the AI re-reads the Data lines against the app: new fields, new destinations (`fetch`, `codex exec`, a new host), new paths; then writes `Data lines checked: <date>`. That is the "every ~5 changes" trigger; the number 5 is a convenience, not from any standard [INFERENCE].
5. One AI-only file, `data-checklist.md`, under 40 lines, is enough; no user-facing document, no policy, no register.

### D. What not to do

- No management system, risk register, statement of applicability, policy set, training, audit or certification. They serve organisations, not one person's tools [1][2].
- No classification labels beyond the three cases "Whose data" already gives.
- No RPO/RTO or business-impact questions to the user. The AI states the worst case and moves on; it asks only "how much would it hurt to lose this?" if the interview did not already answer it [09 rec 16].
- No disk-encryption check by the AI (unreliable, see the gap table) and no attempt to turn it on; that needs admin rights, and "never work around a block" holds [R4].
- No promise of erasure from every copy. The tool can remove data from its own folder and its own copies; files the user already exported or shared are out of its reach, and the message to the user should say that in a line when items 8 and 9 run.

## Where the 3-2-1 rule comes from

The earliest source I could read that states it is the US-CERT paper "Data Backup Options" (Ruggiero and Heckathorn, Carnegie Mellon, 2012): keep 3 copies (1 primary, 2 backups), on 2 different media types, with 1 copy offsite. It cites Peter Krogh, *The DAM Book: Digital Asset Management for Photographers*, 2nd edition, O'Reilly, 2009, p. 207 [23]. Krogh's own site says the book was first published by O'Reilly in 2005 [24, search excerpt]. Blog posts say Krogh coined the rule while writing the first edition, from a podcast page I could not open (HTTP 403) [UNVERIFIED]. So: a photographers' rule of thumb, not an ISO or NIST control. The same paper lists "keeping primary file copies and backup copies on the same internal hard drive" as a con, because one damaged or stolen drive loses both [23].

## Open questions

- All 27002 wording is second-hand [3][4][5][6]. ISO's preview pages should be read once in a browser to confirm the titles used in the "From" column. 29100:2024 is listed at CHF 0 [7], so its real text is worth one read.
- Whether the user wants item 4. It is the only new check that costs them a decision. The default when they decline is: the automatic copy stays, and NOTES says `Copy elsewhere: declined`.
- The retention default ("until the role is filled") is a suggestion, not a legal period; no source gives one. The company's rule wins, and the message to IT asks for it [R1].
- Jurisdiction. If the user is outside the EU or UK the duties change; the checks stay useful [UNVERIFIED].
- The quarterly restore check comes from CIS 11.5, which is only in Implementation Groups 2-3 [21]; the smallest group does not ask for a restore test at all, while NIST, NCSC and ISO do [17][22][4]. I chose to follow the stricter group for data that cannot be rebuilt.
- NIST SP 1300's "Prioritize regularly backing up your data and testing your backups" comes from a search excerpt of the PDF, which I did not read in full [18].
- The ISO/IEC 27031, 27040 and 27005 abstracts come from vendor pages or search excerpts, not ISO's own pages, which returned 403 [11][12][15].
